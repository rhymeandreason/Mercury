Sound design: interaction states

Seven sounds built from one shared set of notes. They fall into three families that each share a character, so users learn the kind of event before they learn each individual sound.

Device: crisp and tactile Treatment: soft and calm Power: rising and falling slides

Design principles

Direction carries meaning

Rising means starting, connecting or gaining. Falling means draining and needing attention. Settling on the high home note means done. Battery Low is the only sound that ends lower than it began.

Families by character

  • Device (On, Dock): quick plucks that feel immediate and mechanical.
  • Treatment (Start, Finished): gentle fade-ins, because the device is being worn.
  • Power (Charger, Full, Low): each starts with a slide, like energy flowing.

Priority

  • Alert (Battery Low): loudest, and repeats to get attention.
  • Notify (Treatment Finished, Fully Charged): moderate; may play while the user is away.
  • Confirm (On, Dock, Start, Charger): quietest and shortest, responding to an action the user just took.

Timing & repetition

  • Confirmations are short enough to land while the hand is still on the device.
  • Battery Low plays at a few thresholds and never loops, because nagging teaches people to ignore it.
  • Treatment Finished repeats once if nobody responds.

Loudness & duration (technical)

Alert 0 dB · Notify −3 dB · Confirm −6 dB. Confirmations ≤ 300 ms; status changes 600–1,200 ms. Only Battery Low may interrupt another sound. Battery Low at 20/10/5%; Finished repeats once after 60 s untouched.

Hardware notes

  • Palette: C‑major pentatonic, 1.0–2.6 kHz. Shift toward the speaker's resonant peak (often 2–4 kHz) for loudness.
  • PWM buzzers can't shape envelopes: step duty cycle (50% → 25% → 10%) to approximate decays, or drop them.
  • Glides on PWM: step frequency every 5–10 ms.
  • Pair each sound with a matching LED/haptic pattern for silent mode.
